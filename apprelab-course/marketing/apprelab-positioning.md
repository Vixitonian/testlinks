# Apprelab Positioning and Marketing Strategy

_Written from the product overview of 4 October 2026. Every product claim below is live today unless it says "planned". Use this file as the messaging source for every Apprelab script and video._

---

## 1. Why Apprelab is hard to market (the honest diagnosis)

Apprelab is four products in one: a course marketplace, a creator tool, a training platform for organisations, and (planned) a talent and work platform. It also has a long feature list: AI feedback, badges, portfolios, bootcamps, live classes, group chats, discounts, offline lessons.

When you try to say all of that, people hear none of it. "A mobile-first micro-learning marketplace with AI feedback and verified badges" describes the machine, not anyone's life. Nobody wakes up wanting a micro-learning marketplace.

The fix is the same method Apprelab teaches its mentors: **one person, one problem, one promise.** Pick who you talk to first, say one thing to them, and let the other features show up as proof.

---

## 2. The market as it really is (Nigeria first)

These are observations of how people already behave, not statistics. Check any number before it goes into a script.

| What people do today | What is broken about it | The opening for Apprelab |
|---|---|---|
| Experts sell "classes" in WhatsApp and Telegram groups, paid by bank transfer with a screenshot as receipt | No structure, no way to check who did the work, endless "abeg resend the link", no proof for the learner, money chased by DM | A real course with tasks, marking, payment and a badge, without the expert becoming a tech company |
| Learners watch YouTube and free courses | They watch, they don't do; nobody checks their work; they finish with nothing to show | Every lesson ends with a task that is marked in seconds |
| People collect certificates of attendance | Employers and clients don't trust them; anyone can print one | A badge only issued when every task passed, with a public page showing the evidence and a QR code |
| Job seekers say "I can do Excel / design / social media" | Saying is not showing | A portfolio of real work and verified badges, shareable on WhatsApp and LinkedIn |
| Schools, NGOs and programmes run training | Attendance is tracked, results are not; reports are done by hand | Private courses and bootcamps with progress reports per person |
| Data and time are expensive | Long videos and long courses get abandoned | Lessons of 3 to 7 minutes, made for phones, downloadable for offline use |

**The cultural key: apprenticeship.** Across Nigeria, people already trust one way of learning a trade: you learn under a master, by doing real work, until you can prove you are ready (tailoring, hairdressing, mechanics, trading). Apprelab's name already carries this idea: **apprentice + lab.** It does not need explaining. It needs naming.

## 3. The positioning

### 3.1 The big idea

> **Apprelab is the digital apprenticeship: learn by doing real work, get it checked, and walk away with proof.**

Short version for the ear: **"Learn it. Do it. Prove it."**

This separates Apprelab from everything people know:

| They are | Apprelab is |
|---|---|
| YouTube and free videos: you watch | You **do**: every lesson ends with a real task |
| Online course sites: you get a certificate | You get **proof**: a verified badge with the work behind it |
| WhatsApp classes: you hope the teacher replies | Your work is **checked in seconds**, every time |
| Long programmes: weeks before you see anything | **3 to 7 minute** lessons, one clear result per course |

### 3.2 Positioning statement (internal, never read aloud)

For **people with a skill to teach or a skill to prove**, mostly in Nigeria and mostly on phones, **Apprelab** is the **learn-by-doing platform** that turns short lessons into **real, checked work and verified proof**. Unlike YouTube, certificate sites and WhatsApp classes, **every lesson ends with a task that is marked in seconds, and every finished course ends with a badge employers and clients can verify.**

### 3.3 Who to talk to first (the beachhead)

A marketplace needs sellers before buyers. Lead with **creators (mentors)** for three reasons:

1. **Each creator brings their own audience.** A designer with 3,000 Instagram followers or an accountant with five WhatsApp groups markets their own course. Every mentor becomes a sales channel.
2. **The pain is sharp and local.** Selling classes on WhatsApp is messy, and everyone who has tried it knows it.
3. **The offer is unusually strong:** keep 85% of every sale, get paid in Naira through Paystack, get a free course that shows you how to build your course, and an AI builder that drafts lessons from your notes.

Then **learners** arrive through creators' links and through shared badges. **Organisations** are a separate, direct-sales track: fewer, larger deals.

| Order | Audience | One promise | Why now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Creators**: practitioners who already get asked for help (designers, marketers, accountants, developers, business owners, tutors) | **"Turn what you know into a course that sells, and keep 85%."** | Supply first; each creator brings buyers |
| 2 | **Learners**: students, graduates, job seekers, workers who want a better job or side income | **"Learn a skill in short lessons on your phone, and walk away with proof you can show."** | They come through creators and shared badges |
| 3 | **Organisations**: schools, universities, companies, NGOs, programmes, hubs | **"Run training you can measure: courses, bootcamps and reports in one place."** | Bigger revenue per deal; sold person to person |
| Later | **Employers and partners** | "Hire people who have already proved it." | Wait until WorkLab is live; do not promise it now |

## 4. Messaging by audience

Each audience gets one promise, three proof points (all live), the main doubt answered, and the words they use.

### 4.1 Creators (lead audience)

- **Promise:** Turn what you know into a course that sells, and keep 85% of every sale.
- **The problem in their words:** "People keep asking me for help for free." "My WhatsApp class is stressful." "I don't know how to make a course." "I have the skill but I'm not making money from it."
- **Proof points:**
  1. You keep **85%** of every sale, paid in **Naira** to your bank through **Paystack** (card, transfer, USSD).
  2. A free Apprelab course walks you through it: find a real customer problem, write the promise, plan 3 to 8 lessons, set the price, and end with a one-page course plan.
  3. The **AI course builder** turns your notes into an outline and lesson drafts; every lesson gets a task that is **marked automatically**, so you are not grading at midnight.
- **Extra proof:** run sales and discount codes yourself; run your own bootcamps with live classes and group chats; see completion rates and sales.
- **Main doubt:** "Will anybody buy?" **Answer:** you build for one person you can call today, and every finished learner shares a badge that links back to your course.
- **Line to remember:** **"Your skill is asleep. Wake it up."**

### 4.2 Learners

- **Promise:** Learn a skill in short lessons on your phone, and walk away with proof you can show.
- **The problem in their words:** "I've watched so many videos." "I have certificates but no job." "Nobody believes me when I say I can do it." "I don't have time or data for long courses."
- **Proof points:**
  1. Lessons are **3 to 7 minutes**, built for phones; download them and learn offline.
  2. Every lesson ends with a **real task**, marked in seconds with two strengths and up to three fixes. You fix it and try again.
  3. Pass every task and you earn a **verified badge** with a public page, the evidence and a QR code. Share it on WhatsApp and LinkedIn, and show your work on your portfolio.
- **Price story:** many courses are free; paid courses start at ₦2,200; the Learner pass is ₦1,500 for 30 days of unlimited AI feedback on free courses.
- **Main doubt:** "Is this another useless certificate?" **Answer:** the badge is issued only when every task passed, anyone can check the evidence behind it, and about one in ten is spot-checked by a person.
- **Line to remember:** **"Don't say you can. Show it."**

### 4.3 Organisations

- **Promise:** Run training you can measure: courses, bootcamps and reports in one place.
- **The problem in their words:** "We know who attended, not who learned." "Reports take forever." "Our programme needs evidence for sponsors."
- **Proof points:**
  1. Private courses for your members, free to them, assigned with due dates; invite up to 500 people at once.
  2. **Bootcamps** with live classes, reminders, group chats (voice notes, files), free or paid through your own Paystack account.
  3. **Reports** per person and per course, filterable by bootcamp and sponsor, plus verified badges as evidence.
- **Main doubt:** "Will our people actually finish?" **Answer:** short lessons, a task in each, streaks and reminders, and you see exactly who is stuck and where.
- **Line to remember:** **"Training you can prove."**

## 5. Brand voice and words

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| learn by doing, real task, checked in seconds, proof, verified badge, show your work | micro-learning marketplace, upskilling, edtech, leverage, empower, revolutionise |
| short lessons on your phone, three to seven minutes | "bite-sized content", "learning journey" |
| keep 85%, paid in Naira | "monetise your expertise", "passive income" |
| digital apprenticeship, learn like an apprentice | "AI-powered" as a headline (say what the AI does instead: "marks your work in seconds") |
| your customer, your class, your WhatsApp group | "your community", "your audience" (too abstract) |

**Voice:** a friendly senior colleague who has done it, not a guru. Plain Nigerian English, warm, direct, a little humour. Concrete: naira amounts, minutes, real situations (market, office, campus, church, WhatsApp). No hype: never "become a millionaire", never "guaranteed job".

| Safe to say (live) | Never say |
|---|---|
| Mentors keep 85% of every sale (Paystack's fee comes from the creator's share) | "Keep 100%", or any income figure for a mentor |

| Refunds within 7 days if no more than two lessons are done | |

## 7. Growth loops (how the message spreads)

1. **Creator loop:** a creator publishes a course and shares the link (WhatsApp Status, groups, Instagram). The link preview shows the course and price. Their followers enrol.
2. **Badge loop:** a learner passes, shares the badge on WhatsApp and LinkedIn. Each badge links back to the course. Their friends see real proof and click.
3. **Creator-from-learner loop:** a learner who finishes thinks "I could teach this", applies, and takes the free mentor course.
4. **Organisation loop:** a school or programme runs a bootcamp; members earn badges; the organisation shows the reports to sponsors and runs the next cohort.

Every script should end on an action that feeds one of these loops: start a course, share a badge, apply to teach, or talk to us about training.

## 8. Channels that fit the local market

| Channel | Use it for | Format |
|---|---|---|
| **WhatsApp** (Status, groups, broadcast lists, channels) | Everything; this is where Nigerians share | 30-second vertical videos with burned-in captions; course and badge links (the previews carry the price) |
| **Instagram and TikTok** | Creators and young learners | 15 to 30 second hooks, creator stories, "watch me fix this task" |
| **LinkedIn** | Badges, professionals, organisations | Badge shares, mentor stories, short explainers for HR and programme leads |
| **X** | The Nigerian tech and creator crowd | Threads and clips on "WhatsApp classes vs real courses" |
| **Telegram** | The Apprelab community group | Launch news, creator tips |
| **Campuses** | Final-year students, NYSC corps members, SIWES students | Ambassadors, free starter courses, badge challenges |
| **Hubs, associations, NGOs, programmes** | Organisations | Person-to-person demos; a pilot bootcamp |
| **Search and blog** | People already looking ("how to sell an online course in Nigeria") | The five drafted blog posts, published, each with an explainer video |

Paid ads only after the organic message is proven: boost the creator videos that already perform.

## 10. What to measure

| Stage | Measure | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Creators | Mentor applications, mentor-course completions, courses published, first sale per mentor | Supply drives everything |
| Learners | Course page visits from shared links, enrolments, first task submitted, badges issued, badges shared | Doing and proving is the product |

| Videos | Watch-through at 3 seconds and at the end, link clicks, WhatsApp shares | Which hooks and stories work |

## 11. First 90 days

| Weeks | Focus | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | **Lock the message** | Adopt this positioning; update the homepage headline to "Learn it. Do it. Prove it." with the creator offer close behind; publish the drafted blog posts |
| 2 to 6 | **Founding creators** | Personally recruit 20 to 30 practitioners who already teach on WhatsApp or Instagram; take them through the mentor course; give each a launch discount code; release the "Wake up your skill" and "Stop teaching in WhatsApp groups" videos |
| 4 to 10 | **Learner proof** | Help founding creators launch; run a "badge challenge" on campuses and in NYSC and SIWES circles; collect real badge stories (with permission) for testimonial videos |
| 6 to 12 | **First organisations** | Two or three pilot bootcamps with a hub, a school and an NGO; turn their reports into a case-study video |
| Ongoing | **Learn and double down** | Weekly: which hooks and creators drive enrolments; boost what works; retire what doesn't |

## 12. One-page summary

- **Big idea:** the digital apprenticeship. **Learn it. Do it. Prove it.**
- **Lead audience:** creators. **Promise:** turn what you know into a course that sells, and keep 85%.
- **Second:** learners. **Promise:** short lessons on your phone, real tasks, proof you can show.
- **Third:** organisations. **Promise:** training you can prove.
- **Enemy:** watching without doing, certificates without proof, WhatsApp classes without structure.
- **Proof, never hype:** only live features, real numbers from the guardrails table, no income or job promises, no WorkLab promises until it ships.
